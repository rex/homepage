## What happened

On a Saturday, the venue's virtual SAN failed over, and when the dust settled the venue's
RabbitMQ wouldn't start. That should have been a quick page. It wasn't, because the page
never broke through my phone's silent mode. Fifty-four missed calls. For about two hours,
nobody could reach me.

## How I made it worse

To rebuild the broker, I pulled the venue out of Rancher's multi-cluster RabbitMQ app and
added it back. Simple enough. Except Rancher's upgrade form defaults the chart version to
*latest*, and I didn't change it. So instead of rebuilding what we had, I deployed a much
newer Bitnami chart that we'd never tested. It failed with `load_definition.json` not found,
and with a secret that didn't contain the key `rabbitmq-password`.

{/* CONFIRM (Pierce): one source says the "secret does not contain key" error showed up at every venue, which would mean the upgrade reached the whole multi-cluster app. The other says the config matched the healthy venues. Settle whether other venues were touched. */}

## The turn

At 5 p.m. I compared the broken venue's config with the healthy venues'. Identical. So the
config wasn't the problem. If everything I *had* configured matched, the difference had to be
something I hadn't: the version. I pinned the chart back to the previous version, and at
5:07 p.m. everything went green at once.

## What changed

- **Paging:** I fixed my phone's bypass settings and reworked how pages reach me. Fifty-four
  missed calls is a lesson you only need once.
- **Automated health checks** on the message broker, so a broken broker pages someone instead
  of waiting to be noticed.
- **A runbook** for rebuilding the broker.
- **Explicit chart versions:** I pin the chart version on every Rancher app edit. The form's
  default is not a decision I get to skip.

## What I'd tell someone else

When the config matches a working system, stop staring at the config and compare versions.
And a default in a UI is still a change. It's just one you didn't mean to make.
